Why SSEG commissioning matters

Small-scale embedded generation (SSEG) is any generator connected to your side of the meter: Rooftop solar PV, a hybrid inverter with batteries, or a generator that can run in parallel with the grid. The utility needs proof that it is safe for the people working on its network, that it disconnects when the grid goes down, and that it behaves as the application says it will.

That proof is the commissioning report. Until it is accepted, the system is not authorised to operate, and an unauthorised system can mean penalties, a disconnected supply or a refused feed-in tariff.

Who it's for

Homeowners whose installer has finished but whose application is stuck. Business owners with rooftop PV, backup batteries and generators. Industrial sites with larger arrays, export limiting and protection settings to prove. And installers who need an independent registered professional to sign their commissioning reports.

What to have ready

  • Your SSEG application or reference number from the City, municipality or Eskom
  • The installer's Certificate of Compliance for the installation
  • A single-line diagram of the system as installed
  • Inverter make and model, and its NRS 097-2-1 certificate
  • Battery and backup details, and whether the system exports
  • Safe access to the inverter, DB boards and the point of connection

SSEG commissioning questions

Is SSEG commissioning required in Cape Town?

Yes. Grid-connected small-scale embedded generation in Cape Town must be authorised by the City before it operates, including systems that don't export. A commissioning report signed by a registered professional is part of that process.

Who can sign the SSEG commissioning report?

The City of Cape Town asks for an ECSA-registered professional. Bramwill Bruinders is a registered Pr. Tech. Eng., and his sign-off is accepted by the City of Cape Town.

Can you commission a system someone else installed?

Yes. Most of our SSEG work is on systems installed by others. The installer stays responsible for the installation and the Certificate of Compliance; we test, report and sign.

What happens if the system fails a test?

You get a plain list of what must be fixed before a re-test. We have no stake in who fixes it.

Do you commission SSEG outside Cape Town?

Yes, for Eskom customers and other municipalities in the Western Cape and across South Africa. Requirements differ by utility, and we work to the one your system connects to.

What does SSEG commissioning cost?

It depends on the system size, battery and backup arrangement, and location. Send us the system size, inverter model and your municipality, and we will quote on a written scope.
