Energy Loss Audit

Your biggest controllable cost is leaking, and nobody is measuring it.

Electricity is the largest operating cost most commercial and industrial owners don't actively manage. The US EPA estimates that around 30% of the energy used in commercial buildings is wasted. In South Africa that waste gets dearer every year: NERSA approved a further 9.01% increase in what Eskom charges municipalities from July 2026, and it flows straight through to municipal tariffs.

Roughly 0.94 kg of CO₂ for every kWh drawn from the national grid. Wasted electricity is not only a cost line. It is most of your carbon footprint.

Where the money leaks

  • After-hours base load. Air conditioning, lighting, compressors and process equipment running when nobody is there.
  • Maximum demand peaks. One badly timed start-up can set your demand charge for the whole month.
  • Poor power factor. Reactive energy and kVA charges for current that does no useful work.
  • HVAC and refrigeration. Wrong setpoints, poor scheduling, and chillers or units fighting each other.
  • Compressed air. Leaks and over-pressure, often the most expensive utility on an industrial site.
  • Motors, pumps and fans. Oversized and running flat out with no variable speed drive.
  • The network itself. Overloaded cables, ageing transformers, harmonics and phase imbalance turning power into heat.
  • Lighting and controls. Old fittings and no occupancy or daylight control.

How an Energy Loss Audit works

  1. Bills and tariff review. Twelve to twenty-four months of accounts, your tariff, time-of-use periods, notified maximum demand and reactive energy charges.
  2. Measure. Power quality logging at the main incomer and key distribution boards, usually over one to two weeks, to build your real load profile.
  3. Load flow and loss analysis. Where the energy goes, board by board, and where the electrical network itself loses it.
  4. Feasibility study. Every saving measure costed, with expected kWh and kVA savings, payback period and carbon reduction.
  5. Proposal and verification plan. A ranked plan you can take to the board, and a measurement method so savings are proven, not promised.

SSEG commissioning

Your solar system is installed. Now it needs to be allowed onto the grid.

A grid-tied PV or battery system has to be commissioned and signed off before the utility grants permission to connect. Eskom and most municipalities ask for the commissioning tests to be signed by an ECSA-registered professional. We do that work for homes, businesses and industrial sites, and our Pr. Tech. Eng. sign-off is accepted by the City of Cape Town.

Homeowners

Your installer has finished and the inverter is running, but the municipality has not approved the connection yet. We check the paperwork, test the system on site, and sign the commissioning report so your application can go in complete the first time.

Business owners

Offices, retail, farms and hospitality sites with rooftop PV, backup batteries and generators. We test how the inverter, the backup supply and the grid behave together, so the system that saves you money doesn't become the one that trips you offline.

Industrial sites

Larger arrays, export limiting, protection settings and interfaces with existing MV and LV distribution. We commission against the utility's requirements and report what your engineering team needs for the long run, not just what the application form asks for.

Document check
Single-line diagram, inverter approval and NRS 097-2-1 certificate, protection settings, export or non-export configuration, and the application pack.
On-site tests
Loss-of-mains (anti-islanding) behaviour, export limiting where required, isolation and protection, earthing and bonding, DC isolation, backup changeover and labelling.
Professional sign-off
The commissioning report completed and signed as a registered Pr. Tech. Eng., ready to submit with your installer's Certificate of Compliance.
A straight answer if it isn't ready
A plain list of what must be fixed before a re-test. We have no stake in who fixes it.

The installer and the registered electrical contractor stay responsible for the installation and the CoC. The utility grants permission to connect.

Questions we're often asked

What is an energy loss audit?

An energy audit focused on finding where a commercial or industrial building loses electricity and money. We review your bills and tariff, log the load at the incomer and main boards, run a load flow and loss analysis, and hand you a costed, ranked plan of savings measures with payback and carbon impact.

Should I do an energy audit before installing solar?

Yes. Every kWh of waste you remove is a kWh you no longer need to generate, store or back up. Cutting base load and demand peaks first usually means a smaller PV system and battery for the same result, and a faster payback.

Who can sign off an SSEG commissioning report in Cape Town?

The City of Cape Town asks for the SSEG commissioning report to be signed by an ECSA-registered professional. Bramwill Bruinders is a registered Professional Engineering Technologist (Pr. Tech. Eng.), and his sign-off is accepted by the City of Cape Town.

What does an independent electrical compliance review cover?

Within a written scope, we review project requirements, contractor documentation, design and test evidence, site observations and handover records against the agreed project criteria. You get the gaps, the residual risks and the close-out actions, with a clear recommendation. It is advisory, not a statutory approval or a Certificate of Compliance.

Do you sell or install equipment?
