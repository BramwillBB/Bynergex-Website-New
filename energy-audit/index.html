Energy Loss Audit

Find where your electricity goes, what it costs, and whether your supply is fit for your equipment.

Every Bynergex Energy Loss Audit measures both the quantity and the quality of the electricity on your site. You receive a costed, ranked savings plan and a power quality screening from a single site visit.

Most commercial buildings pay for electricity that does no useful work

After-hours base load, a badly timed start-up that sets the month's demand charge, poor power factor and equipment fighting each other do not appear as line items on the bill. Neither do harmonic currents that overheat cables and transformers, or voltage unbalance that shortens motor life.

These losses are real, measurable and often expensive. Most audits only look at the first group. We measure both.

What the audit covers

We start with your bills and finish with a plan you can act on.

  1. Tariff and bill review. We review at least 12 months of electricity accounts and confirm that you are on the most suitable tariff.
  2. Load logging. We install a power quality analyser at the main incomer for a minimum of 7 days to record how and when the site uses electricity.
  3. Maximum demand analysis. We identify the events that set your monthly demand charge and how to reduce them.
  4. Base load analysis. We measure what the site consumes when it should be idle.
  5. Power factor assessment. We quantify reactive energy and its effect on kilovolt-ampere (kVA) charges.
  6. Loss identification. We identify where energy is wasted across major loads and systems.
  7. Costed savings plan. Each recommendation includes estimated savings, capital cost, payback period and carbon impact.

Power Quality Screening, included in every audit

The same analyser that records your consumption also records the condition of your supply. We analyse that data and report on it as part of every audit, at no extra charge.

What we measure

  • Root mean square (RMS) voltage level and variation
  • Voltage unbalance between phases
  • Voltage and current harmonic distortion, total and by individual order
  • Flicker, short-term (Pst) and long-term (Plt)
  • Voltage dips, swells and interruptions
  • True power factor and displacement power factor
  • Neutral current

How we report it

We compare the measured voltage quality against the limits in NRS 048-2 and estimate the energy and cost impact wherever power quality contributes to losses or charges. The audit report includes a one-page summary that rates each parameter as Pass, Watch or Action.

Energy loss audit and power quality assessment: What is the difference?

The two studies use similar instruments but answer different questions. An energy loss audit asks how much electricity the site uses, where it goes and what it costs. A power quality assessment asks whether the electricity is stable and clean enough for your equipment to work properly.

Why we combine them

Poor power quality causes energy losses, and energy efficiency measures can create power quality problems. Treating them separately leaves gaps.

  1. Harmonics waste energy. Harmonic currents increase the current in cables, transformers and motors without doing useful work. The result is extra heat and extra cost.
  2. Voltage unbalance increases motor losses. A small unbalance in voltage produces a much larger unbalance in current, which raises motor temperatures and reduces efficiency.
  3. Savings measures can introduce new problems. Variable speed drives, light-emitting diode (LED) lighting and solar inverters all generate harmonics. Power factor correction capacitors can resonate with existing harmonics if they are not correctly specified.
  4. Better recommendations. When we recommend power factor correction, drives or solar, we specify them with your site's actual harmonic and voltage conditions in mind.
  5. One site visit, one report. You receive a complete picture from a single measurement campaign, without appointing two separate studies.
  6. A baseline for verification. The combined data set allows a measured before-and-after comparison once improvements or solar systems are installed.

What the screening does not cover

The included screening identifies whether a problem exists and how serious it is. It is not a full investigation. The following items are outside the scope of the screening and are available through a Detailed Power Quality Investigation:

  • Measurements at more than one point on the installation
  • Root-cause investigation of individual loads
  • Design or specification of harmonic filters or power factor correction
  • Formal compliance reports or evidence for supply quality disputes with the utility
  • Assessment of customer emission limits under NRS 048-4

Note: Screening results are measured on the customer side of the main incomer and are indicative. A formal assessment at the point of supply requires a dedicated measurement campaign.

Frequently asked questions

Is the power quality screening an extra cost?

No. The screening is included in every Energy Loss Audit. A Detailed Power Quality Investigation is quoted separately if you need one.

Why do you log for at least 7 days?

A full week captures weekday and weekend operating patterns. It also matches the minimum assessment period used for voltage quality under NRS 048-2.

Will the screening prove that my supply is out of specification?

It shows whether voltage quality at your incomer falls within the NRS 048-2 limits and flags any concerns. A formal supply quality dispute with the utility requires a detailed investigation with a measurement campaign designed for that purpose.

Do you sell or install equipment?

No. Bynergex Consulting is an advisory-only firm. We do not supply equipment, install systems or accept supplier commissions, so our recommendations serve the client.

What do you need from us before the site visit?

At least 12 months of electricity accounts, access to the main distribution board, a single-line diagram if one exists, and a contact person on site.

Do you work outside Cape Town?

We are based in Cape Town and serve clients across the Western Cape. Work elsewhere in South Africa is available by arrangement.
